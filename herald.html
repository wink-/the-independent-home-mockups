Front page

Solar year clears 71% of house use, with two invoices the paper wishes it had printed correctly

One year, one array, one longer winter than the spreadsheet accounted for.

By the property · Tuesday, September 29, 2026

The array, drawn from the kitchen window, with the sun on its second lap of the year. Watercolour, one sitting.

We are one year into the array, and the year is at last complete. The panels made 8.9 megawatt-hours of power, which is 71 per cent of what the house used. The remaining 29 per cent came off the grid, almost all of it in January, when the trees win and the meter runs faster than the inverter.

It cost $2,940 out of pocket after the credit. The paper has this number down because every invoice was written down, including the two the property would rather not have printed. The credit took eleven weeks. The trenching took longer than the wiring. Both are mentioned, in the corrections panel below.

If the house can make power and cost less to run, then work gets a little less powerful over the life of the property. That is the whole plan, and it is not a secret one. It is also not free, and the property would like it known that it is not free.

Two of the six garden beds paid for themselves in groceries. The other four paid for themselves in lessons, principally the lesson that you should buy the dirt after you build the bed.

Dispatch 06

Repair kit posts a net number, at last

Fourteen jobs this year. Gross looked fine. Net after parts, tires and one free redo came to $1,905. The free redo is the only job the property would not redo differently, given the same information.

Dispatch 11

Rain barrel test enters phase two

1,100 gallons caught, one shed flooded, one overflow learned. The property is shopping for a second barrel and a longer downspout. The guttering remains unfinished.

Dispatch 03
Corrections

“The paper regrets the errors, and is better for knowing.”

  1. In the August 9 report on the $412 bench, the planer blade was listed at $34. It was, in fact, $34 and a weekend. The paper regrets the omitted weekend.
  2. A reader writes to point out that the dirt in the garden beds ($210) was not, in fact, needed. The property agrees, and stands corrected.
  3. The tape measure is accurate to four feet. Earlier reports suggesting otherwise referred to the same tape measure, left in the rain. The property stands by it, and by the four feet.
  4. We are asked to clarify that the lent-out 3/8 inch bit is, in fact, lent out. The earlier reference to it being off the wall since March is correct, and is not in dispute.

— The Editor, on behalf of the property.

Ask the property

Q.I am thinking about a second solar array this winter. Should I price it the way I priced the first one?

A.Include the trenching. The trenching is where the last number went wrong, and the number you do not write down is the number you remember.

Q.How do you know when a project is done?

A.When the cost stops being interesting. It is not done when the win is. The win is when the project is interesting. The cost is when it becomes a project.
